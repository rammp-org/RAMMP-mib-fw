Drive boards

Each wheel is its own PACE RACER board. The page reads every MotorState the boards publish at 20 Hz and writes MotorTuning straight to them over the same network the MIB uses; nothing goes through the MIB. The MIB only has to keep driving. A board that never appears here is off, not discovered (switch multicast; see the README), or has no axis set on its console.

kp 2.39, ki 4.77, PLL 10 are the shipped defaults; the hub motor makes 2.6× the torque per amp, so expect to soften them
Apply sends the three values once; the board applies them on its next tick without resetting the integrator, so it is safe under way. Enter 0 for kp or ki to switch that term off (sent as the API's "exactly 0", which needs board API v3). The board clamps each value into its compile-time range; read the "in force" column rather than assuming. Nothing survives the board's power cycle until it is saved to NVS, and Save writes whatever is in force at that moment. Order for a wheel that shakes: halve ki, then halve kp, then lower the PLL toward 5 (smoother, more lag) or raise it toward 20 if the loop hunts slowly.

A step latches the stick at that value until Stop, so the chassis keeps moving: wheels off the ground first. A pulse latches it for the given time and the server releases it on a timer, so the target goes high, holds, and returns to zero on its own; the metrics table then reports both edges. The target the board tracks is the MIB's command after its trajectory planner, so the dashed line ramps at the profile's acceleration limit rather than stepping. "Square it" sets the active profile's linear and angular acceleration parameters to their maximum for the pulse and puts them back a second after it ends; it needs the Parameters page's firmware, and stays greyed out without it. The board itself steps the reference because the MIB sends no accel_limit; a stepped reference under a 0.5 integrator is the first cause of shaking.

Computed from the trace for the last two target changes, so a pulse shows its rising and falling edge. A step starts where the target begins to change and the step size is the target it settles at. Rise is measured on the wheel, from 10 % to 90 % of the step. Overshoot is the peak past the final target as a share of the step. Settled is the time after which the wheel stays within 5 % of the step for half a second. Steady error is the mean target-minus-measured over the last half second once settled.
